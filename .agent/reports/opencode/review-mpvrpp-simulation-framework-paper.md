# Review Report: "A Simulation Framework for the Multi-Period Vehicle Routing Problem with Profits in Smart Waste Collection"

**Reviewer**: opencode (GLM-5.3)
**Date**: 2026-08-28
**Artifact reviewed**: `assets/papers/Simulation-Framework-for-the-MPVRP-with-Profits-in-Smart-Waste-Collection/paper.tex` (1,301 lines), all six generated result tables (`Tables/*.tex`), the bibliography (`mybibliography.bib`), the compiled PDF metadata (34 pp.), and cross-checks against the WSmart-Route codebase (`logic/src/policies/`, `logic/gen/gen_paper_latex.py`).

**Scope caveat**: figure PNGs could not be rendered in this session (no image input available). Figures are evaluated on captions, in-text usage, file metadata (dimensions/DPI), and the generation pipeline — not on visual appearance. This is flagged wherever it matters.

---

## 1. Executive Summary

This is a **statistically unusually honest** benchmark paper wrapped around a real, verifiable framework, with genuinely excellent numerical hygiene — I re-derived essentially every count in every table (456 = 8×57; 400 = 80 slices × 5 variants; 224 = 240 − 8 − 8 matched pairs; +0.26 kg/km paired horizon mean; 751/456 km increments; 4.5× runtime spread) and found **no arithmetic errors**. That is rare and deserves explicit credit.

It is also a paper with **one foot in a paper it did not write**: the abstract promises Neural Combinatorial Optimization benchmarks that appear nowhere in the results; the methodology asserts policies see sensed fill levels "never the true ones" while the protocol states the opposite; the profit objective that all eight constructors maximize is never reported in any table; and a "reproducible" framework paper ships without a code availability statement.

Verdict: **major revision** — the scientific core is sound and the integrity machinery is exemplary, but the framing inconsistencies, missing experimental parameters, and reporting gaps would draw fire from any competent reviewer at an OR venue (e.g., VeRoLog, EJOR, COR, or an LNCS conference such as LOD/HIC).

## 3. Strengths (Ranked)

### S1. Statistical integrity as a first-class contribution — exemplary
The whole-cell exclusion logic (Sect. 5.6), the balancing of every marginal comparison (Sect. 5.2–5.4), the refusal to report cross-constructor 90-day comparisons, and the explicit enumeration of three forms of the same selection-bias hazard (Sect. 5.7) are handled more carefully than in most published OR benchmarks. The passage "a filter adopted for a defensible reason is correlated with the factor under comparison and can determine the comparison" (paper.tex:1148–1150) is the kind of sentence rarely seen in this literature. The detection rule for degenerate runs (tonnage shortfall with a 0.00-median / ≤0.071 background against 0.30–0.86 outliers) is clean and convincing.

### S2. The paired demand-realization design
Re-seeding waste generation per policy-and-day so that every algorithm faces literally identical bin-level increments is the correct way to make multi-period stochastic comparisons, and the paper both implements it and explains why it matters (tonnage comparability within scenario). The single-bin fill trajectory figure (Fig. `fig:fill`), showing the same bin under identical increments with only collection timing varying, is a genuinely insightful visualization choice.

### S3. Honest epistemic labeling
Nearly every quantitative claim is tagged with its inferential status: "descriptive contrasts between configured variants, not a controlled comparison" (paper.tex:873), "not estimates over an unspecified population of algorithms" (paper.tex:798–799), "it is not evidence for that claim over the unobserved policies" (paper.tex:956–957). The improver section openly reports that its own experiment cannot identify the effect it wanted (upstream stochasticity broke pairing). Refusing to oversell is a strength; see W5 for the flip side.

### S4. The three-stage decomposition is a real architectural contribution
The mandatory-selection / construction / improvement split with plugin registries is a clean factorization of the decision space, and it exists in the codebase (`logic/src/policies/mandatory_selection/` with 30+ selection strategies, `route_construction/` with exact/meta/hyper/learning subfolders, `route_improvement/` with 35+ improvers) — the paper's claim that the experiment is a "deliberately small subset" of the policy space is, if anything, an understatement. The framing "selection matters more than construction" (a ~2.8× wider observed efficiency range) is a useful, non-obvious headline finding for benchmark designers.

### S5. Related work is genuinely well organized
The exact-methods subsection (compact vs. decomposition lineages, Ryan–Foster → B&P → BPC, Lysgaard/Jepsen/Gu cuts, ng-routes, Wentges stabilization) is compact and correct, and positioning the four closest waste-collection studies as *components implemented inside the benchmark* rather than external competitors (paper.tex:333–338) is elegant. The ACO-on-the-solution-graph vs. ACO-on-the-operator-graph contrast (PG-CLNS vs. ACO-HH) is a nice didactic touch.

### S6. Real data and real geometry effects
Two municipal networks with road-distance matrices, an empirical bootstrap demand process alongside a parametric one, and — importantly — the identification of the remote-depot geometry (median depot distance ≈ 5× median inter-bin distance) as the mechanism behind the selection ordering. That mechanistic hypothesis, plus its explicit testability caveat (both networks share the geometry, so the mechanism is confounded; paper.tex:1120–1122), is good science.

## 4. Weaknesses (Ranked)

### W1. CRITICAL — The abstract sells results the paper does not contain
The abstract lists "Neural Combinatorial Optimization (NCO)" among the adapted solvers whose "extensive benchmark evaluates the solvers" (paper.tex:91–95), and "Neural Combinatorial Optimization" is a keyword (paper.tex:100). The body then concedes: "Every stored 30- and 90-day benchmark row, however, uses one of the eight classical constructors… the reported results contain no learned-solver observation" (paper.tex:249–252). I understand the abstract is a fixed "abstract of record" tied to a conference presentation (paper.tex:72–77, GitHub issue #53), but a reader who skims the abstract (i.e., most readers) will expect NCO numbers and will not get them. This is the single most likely source of reviewer resentment. The body's disclosure is honest; the abstract's promise is not. If the abstract truly cannot change, the paper needs a prominent early flag — a footnote on the keywords or a first-section sentence ("the NCO adapters are in scope but unexercised; see Sect. 3") — and the authors should reconsider whether the keyword list can drop NCO even if the prose cannot.

### W2. HIGH — Direct internal contradiction on sensing
- Sect. 4.1: "All three strategies decide from **sensed** fill levels, **never from the true ones**" (paper.tex:373–375).
- Sect. 4.4: "for this study we ran the simulations with the **true observations** being given as input to the routing policies" (paper.tex:683–686).
- Fig. `fig:sim_loop` caption: "IoT sensors inject observation noise ε (where, for this study, ε=0)" (paper.tex:662–664).

With ε = 0, "sensed" and "true" coincide, so there is no experimental inconsistency — but there is a glaring *textual* one. Sect. 4.1's "never from the true ones" is flatly false under this protocol. The Limitations section partially repairs this (paper.tex:1193–1199), but Sect. 4.1 must be rewritten to say the strategies are *designed* for sensed levels and *here* receive true levels because ε = 0. As written, a careful reviewer will read either Sect. 4.1 or Sect. 4.4 as describing the wrong experiment.

### W3. HIGH — The optimized objective is never reported, and two declared metrics are missing
Every constructor in the benchmark maximizes $\mathcal{P} = r_w\sum w - c_{km}\sum \text{dist}$ (Eq. 3), yet:
- **No table contains a profit column.** Not once. The algorithms are ranked on kg/km, overflow counts, km, and runtime — none of which is the quantity they were configured to optimize.
- **kg lost** is declared one of the five reported measures (paper.tex:785–787) and then appears in no results table.
- **Collected tonnage (kg)** appears only inside the integrity analysis, never as an outcome.

This objective–metric mismatch has a substantive consequence the paper never confronts: a solver could dominate on kg/km while being worse on the actual profit objective (e.g., by collecting less total waste more efficiently), and the current tables could not reveal it. Either report $\mathcal{P}$ (which requires stating $r_w$ and $c_{km}$ — see W4) or explicitly justify why kg/km is the decision-relevant projection of $\mathcal{P}$ and why the profit ordering is implied by the reported ones (it is not, in general).

### W4. HIGH — Reproducibility gaps despite the "reproducible simulator" claim
For a paper whose first contribution bullet is "A reproducible simulator" (paper.tex:135), the following parameters are absent from the text:
- $r_w$ (revenue/kg) and $c_{km}$ (cost/km) — never given;
- vehicle capacity $Q$ and bin capacities $C_i$ — never given (only Gamma means 6–24 kg/day imply scale indirectly);
- the per-run time budgets for BPC and SWC-TCF ("Both exact methods run under a per-run time budget", paper.tex:474 — *what budget?*);
- the safety coefficient $z$ for Service-Level ("holding $z$ constant" — constant *at what value?*);
- ALNS decay $\lambda$ and segment length, HGS population sizes, SANS cooling schedule, PG-CLNS population/refinement parameters, ACO-HH $\alpha,\beta$;
- number of vehicles $K$ (Eq. 3 admits up to $K$ routes; the experiment is single-vehicle, but $K$ is never formally set to 1);
- the seed / seed-count policy (one realization per configuration — acknowledged, but the seed itself and the seeding procedure for "re-seeds its waste generation per policy-and-day" deserve a precise description, since the entire pairing machinery rests on it).
- **No code availability statement at all.** No URL, no DOI, no "available upon request". For a framework/benchmark paper this is close to disqualifying. The repo exists and the table generator (`logic/gen/gen_paper_latex.py`) is clearly real — say so, and link it.

### W5. MEDIUM — The improver stage yields no interpretable result, and the paper spends 3 pages saying so
The improver comparison (Sect. 5.3.3) is broken by design: upstream stochastic construction did not feed the same routes to both improvers, so 224 "matched" pairs match only the scenario, not the input. The paper says this honestly, repeatedly, and correctly. But the honest conclusion is that **this third of the factorial design produced no usable finding**, and the right response is to fix the experiment (deterministic constructor, or persist constructor output and re-improve the identical plan) rather than to publish an entire subsection of caveats around a number ($\Delta = 0.74$ kg/km, paper.tex:table) that the paper itself says cannot be attributed to the improver. This is where honesty and useful science diverge: reporting an uninterpretable contrast at full length serves neither the reader nor the benchmark. A one-paragraph null report plus the fix in Future Work would be stronger.

### W6. MEDIUM — One number is overstated
"Mandatory selection spans a wider observed efficiency-service range than route construction… The range in mean efficiency across the five tested selection variants is **nearly four times** the corresponding range across constructors" (paper.tex:851–855). From the tables: constructors 6.65−5.36 = 1.29; selections 7.38−3.81 = 3.57; ratio ≈ **2.8**, not "nearly four". (On medians: 3.32/1.06 ≈ 3.1.) The claim's direction is safe and the finding stands, but a reviewer who checks this — I did — will find "nearly four times" unsupported by the paper's own tables. Say "nearly three times" or state the two ranges explicitly.

### W7. MEDIUM — 34 pages for an LNCS-format paper
LNCS conference contributions are typically 12–16 pages plus references; this compiles to 34 pages including a 4-page appendix. Even allowing for a journal-style or extended version, the current text has clear condensation targets:
- Sect. 5.7 (Discussion) restates, nearly verbatim, numbers already given in 5.3.1–5.3.4 (5.9/19.9 overflow spread, 2.4–3.3 horizon ratios, 4.5× runtime spread, 57-per-constructor). The Discussion should interpret, not repeat.
- The related-work taxonomy paragraph (paper.tex:214–234) is beautiful prose but spends ~20 lines defining hyper-heuristics and matheuristics that could be compressed now that Sect. 3.2–3.4 also introduce them.
- The three separate statements of the 90-day selection caveat (Sect. 5.2, 5.3.4, 5.7, plus table footnotes) can be consolidated to one prominent statement plus cross-references.

### W8. MEDIUM — Single demand realization per configuration, no uncertainty quantification
One realization per configuration means every mean in every table has no sampling-error bar, and no significance test is possible or attempted. The paper knows this ("With one realization per configuration, we cannot estimate the selection effect…", paper.tex:970–972) and lists replication as the "obvious next experiment" (paper.tex:1208). But then headline contrasts like "the roughly sevenfold steepening" (paper.tex:1136–1137) rest on differences between two descriptive deltas computed from single-realization means. At minimum, the paper should report the spread of the paired per-configuration differences (not just their mean) so readers can judge whether 340-vs-49 km/overflow survives noise; the underlying per-run data clearly exists.

### W9. LOW-MEDIUM — Figures and tables: craft issues I can verify without seeing them
- **A table shipped as a PNG** (Fig. `fig:app-cls-table`, `appendix_cls_table_30d.png`, 3060×1116) is bad practice in any venue: it cannot be searched, screen-read, or restyled, and its fonts will not match the document. Re-render as LaTeX (`longtable` is already loaded).
- **Raster figures throughout.** Main-text PNGs are 857–1425 px wide; at 0.8–1.0 `\linewidth` that lands near or below 200 DPI in print. Matplotlib outputs should be exported as PDF and included directly (the paper already navigates llncs/caption-package conflicts competently, so vector figures are low-risk).
- **Axis-extent hostage-taking by outliers.** The appendix Pareto figure's caption admits the degenerate 2,168-overflow point "also sets that panel's horizontal axis extent" (paper.tex:1016–1018). Publishing a figure whose scale is set by a point the paper itself excludes from all aggregates is self-inflicted damage: either clip the axis, use a log scale, or plot the excluded point as an off-scale annotation arrow.
- Captions carrying multi-sentence *caveats* (Figs. `fig:app-pareto`, `fig:app-strategy`, `fig:app-heatmaps-90d`, `fig:app-cls-table`) are a symptom: these appendix artifacts contradict the filtered main text and need warnings to be used safely. Consider replacing them with regenerated, integrity-filtered versions — the generator exists.
- The simulation-loop figure describes noise machinery (ε) unused in the study it illustrates (paper.tex:662–664) — same contradiction family as W2.
- The compile is clean (no undefined references or overfull boxes in `paper.log`) — credit — but the document is on **letter paper**; LNCS production expects the class's own geometry. Trivial to fix, easy for a copy-editor to resent.

### W10. LOW — Small errors and typos (line-referenced)
1. paper.tex:1172 — "simulations to be run on **difference** temporal horizons" → "different".
2. paper.tex:1188 — "**a** upstream phase" → "an upstream phase".
3. paper.tex:461 — "while the Restricted Master Problem … is **unfeasible**" → "infeasible" (twice the concept appears; standardize).
4. Table `tab:excluded` — policy label "**SLSL2**" is almost certainly a generation-pipeline naming bug (should be SL2; the "SL" prefix is doubled). A visible data-pipeline defect inside a published table is exactly the kind of thing that undermines confidence in an otherwise immaculate numerical apparatus.
5. Sect. 5.3.4 — the "median 90-day to 30-day ratio lies between 2.4 and 3.3" claim is computed from medians the table does not show (the table's overflow columns are means; mean-derived ratios for ACO-HH are 3.6, already above the stated band). Either add the median-ratio column or state the mean ratios, which span 2.8–3.6 — still "approximately proportional to the horizon", but not the numbers in the prose.
6. Sect. 5.3.1 — "PSOMA and HGS 3 each, BPC 2, and ACO-HH and ALNS 1 each" (Pareto memberships) sums to 5+3+3+2+1+1 = 15 across six scenario-distribution combinations; SWC-TCF and SANS are not mentioned. Say "0 each" or "the remaining two constructors appear on none" — silence is ambiguous.
7. The 174 → 165 horizon configuration drop (paper.tex:777 vs 951) is never explained. By my reconstruction ~9 Pareto-carried configurations must fall in the cells excluded at 30 days; the paper should say so in one sentence, because 174 and 165 sit in different sections with no bridge.
8. Eq. (6), the Service-Level rule: "$\hat{w}_{i,d} + n_d\hat{\mu}_i + z\hat{\sigma}_i\sqrt{n_d} \geq 100\%$" mixes an absolute fill level (kg, per the state definition $w_{i,d} \in [0, C_i]$) with a percentage threshold. Either normalize $\hat w$ by $C_i$ or write $\geq C_i$.
9. Overflow definition drift: Sect. 5.2 defines an overflow event as a bin-day "at capacity" (paper.tex:786–787); Sect. 4.4 defines loss as waste "beyond capacity" with the displayed level capped (paper.tex:689–691). "At" vs. "beyond" matters for whether a full-but-not-overflowing bin counts; pick one and define it once.
10. Branding inconsistency: "WSmartRoute+" (body) vs. "WSmart Route+" (acknowledgments, paper.tex:1225) vs. "WSmart-Route" (repository). Harmless, but sloppy in a paper whose selling point is standardization.
11. Bibliography: `WENTGES2006` carries year 2006 but the DOI (10.1111/j.1475-3995.1997.tb00071.x) and volume 4 are from **1997** — the stabilization reference is misdated. `ma2024learning` is NeurIPS **2023** (key says 2024; text cites it as 2024). `Kool2018AttentionLT` is ICLR **2019** (key says 2018). Individually trivial; collectively they suggest the .bib was never audited.
12. The bib file carries ~30 uncited entries (vaswani2017attention, wu2019graph, kingma2014adam, Paszke2017, etc.) — invisible in the output, but a hazard for the next revision's `\nocite` hygiene.

## 5. Section-by-Section Assessment

### Abstract (paper.tex:78–101)
Competent and clearly written, with a correct problem statement and a real gap ("lack of standardized multi-period evaluation environments"). But it is damaged by W1 (NCO promised, absent) and by the fact that its punchline — the selection-stage dominance finding, which the introduction itself calls "the study's main empirical result" — is entirely absent. The most interesting sentence the paper owns never makes it into the abstract. If the abstract-of-record constraint permits *any* amendment, this is the one to fight for; if not, the mismatch must be managed in the body (W1).

### Introduction (Sect. 1)
The four-sentence cold open (paper.tex:105–109) is excellent — "a useful evaluation must therefore follow a policy over time instead of scoring an isolated route" is the crispest motivation sentence in the paper. The contributions list is honest and matches the content, with one exception: "learned constructors can use the same interface in future comparisons" (paper.tex:146) is doing silent work to excuse the missing NCO results — say the omission plainly here, in one clause, rather than deferring it to Related Work.

### Problem Definition (Sect. 2)
Clean, minimal, correct. The state-transition equation, route structure, and objective are well-formed, and the capacity and no-repeat constraints are correctly scoped (the depot exemption in the $\forall(t,k)\neq(t',k')$ quantifier is handled by the $1\le t\le T$ bounds — I checked). Two gaps: (a) $K$ is never fixed to 1 for the experiments; (b) the cumulative objective silently ignores overflow/loss — the thing every results table measures. A one-sentence acknowledgment that service failure enters evaluation but not the optimization objective (and why — e.g., that a loss penalty $-c_{loss}\sum \ell_{i,d}$ would change the problem class being benchmarked) would close a real conceptual hole.

### Related Work (Sect. 3)
The strongest survey writing in the paper: the guarantee-vs-cost organizing axis, the compact/decomposition lineage, and the "same metaphor one level lower" ACO contrast are all genuinely instructive. Weaknesses: it is long relative to its yield (W7), and it front-loads an NCO taxonomy (~1.5 columns, paper.tex:236–252) for solvers that never appear in the results — defensible as scoping the framework, but in tension with the page budget it is a luxury.

### Methodology (Sect. 4)
The three-stage architecture description is clear and the per-constructor descriptions are, on the whole, remarkably good — the HGS giant-tour/split-decoding explanation and the SWC-TCF two-commodity intuition ("two quantities on every arc whose sum equals vehicle capacity") are textbook-quality. Weaknesses: parameter starvation (W4 — not one numeric setting for any of the eight constructors beyond CF thresholds and $n_d$), the sensing contradiction (W2), and the PG-CLNS description, which as the paper's only *original* algorithm deserves a clearer statement of what is contributed beyond "HVPL-inspired": what specifically is new — the edge-pheromone/global-best-only deposit with per-round penalties? The per-individual ALNS weighting? Name the delta; right now the novel part is discoverable only by diffing against Sun et al. mentally.

### Experimental Evaluation (Sect. 5)
The design description, integrity handling, and balancing are the paper's best material (S1, S2). Specific notes beyond W3/W5/W6/W8:
- The remote-depot geometry observation (paper.tex:746–753) is excellent and should be promoted — it is the *reason* the headline finding exists, and it currently sits buried at the end of the scenarios subsection.
- The strategy table's perfectly monotone ordering across all five measures (efficiency ↓, overflow ↓, km ↑, time ↑) is suspiciously clean — which is itself evidence for how strong the mechanism is, and worth one sentence of remark.
- SL1 vs. Look-Ahead deserve a head-to-head sentence: they sit adjacent on the frontier with nearly identical overflows (3.3 vs. 5.5) but 24% efficiency apart — is LA simply dominated here? The paper never asks dominance questions at the strategy level, only at the constructor level.
- The Empirical-vs-Gamma-3 contrast ("a heavier load: more waste per kilometer, but capacity reached more often", paper.tex:980–983) is fine but thin — this is where per-scenario kg and kg_lost (W3) would have enriched the story at zero experimental cost.

### Discussion (Sect. 5.7)
Intellectually the most valuable section — the "one methodological hazard in three forms" paragraph (paper.tex:1142–1153) should be required reading for benchmark authors. Structurally the weakest: it repeats Results-section numbers verbatim (W7) and its final paragraph (the ablation proposal, paper.tex:1155–1166) overlaps almost entirely with Future Work (paper.tex:1210–1214). Merge them.

### Conclusion / Limitations / Future Work (Sect. 6)
Honest, accurate, and well-aligned with the body's caveats. The Limitations paragraph is genuinely self-critical (single realization, single vehicle, no sensor noise, conditional 90-day sample). Two issues: the Conclusion's second sentence is ungrammatical to the point of obscuring its meaning ("be it during the selection… as well as by enabling simulations to be run on difference temporal horizons", paper.tex:1170–1173 — also typo #1); and the Future Work list is a laundry list of ten items with no prioritization — the replicated factorial design is correctly named "the obvious next experiment"; lead with it, group the rest.

### Appendix
The appendix's reason for existing is unclear: it reproduces *presentation-sourced* figures that include the excluded degenerate runs and therefore require warning labels to read safely (W9). In a page-constrained venue this material would be first against the wall; even in an extended version, regenerating it through the same integrity pipeline as the main text costs the authors an afternoon (the generator exists) and removes three captions-worth of caveats.

## 6. Recommendations

### Must fix (before any submission)
1. **Reconcile the NCO framing**: amend the abstract if permitted; otherwise add an explicit early disclosure in the Introduction and drop NCO from the keywords if the venue allows.
2. **Rewrite Sect. 4.1's sensing sentence** to match the ε = 0 protocol (W2), and regenerate or re-caption the simulation-loop figure accordingly.
3. **Add a code and data availability statement** with a repository URL/DOI and license (W4). This is non-negotiable for a framework paper.
4. **State $r_w$, $c_{km}$, $Q$, bin capacities, exact-solver time budgets, $z$, $K$, and the seeding procedure** (W4), ideally in a parameter table.
5. **Fix the "SLSL2" table label, the "nearly four times" overstatement, and typos #1–#3** (W10).
6. **Report profit (or justify its absence) and kg lost** (W3). At minimum, add a kg column to Table `tab:constructors30` and a kg-lost column to the strategy table — the data exists.

### Should fix
7. Redesign the improver experiment (persist constructor output; re-improve identical plans with both improvers; optionally add a deterministic constructor as a control) and replace the current 3-page caveat-laden subsection with a short, interpretable result (W5).
8. Add spread/uncertainty to the headline contrasts — per-configuration paired differences, at least as box strips in Figs. `fig:strategy` and `fig:improver` (W8).
9. Cut ~30% of the length: de-duplicate Discussion vs. Results vs. Future Work, compress the related-work taxonomy, and fold the appendix into the integrity pipeline or drop it (W7, W9).
10. Explain 174 → 165 (W10.7), fix the median-vs-mean horizon ratio verifiability gap (W10.5), and complete the Pareto-membership enumeration (W10.6).
11. Re-render the appendix CLS table as LaTeX and export all figures as vector PDF (W9).
12. Audit the .bib (W10.11) and normalize the project name (W10.10).

### To make the strongest parts even stronger
13. **Promote the integrity methodology into a named, reusable artifact.** The whole-cell exclusion rule, the balancing-by-construction, and the "hazard in three forms" taxonomy are general contributions to benchmark methodology, not just good practice for this paper. A half-page boxed "protocol" (or an appendix formally stating the exclusion/balancing rules as an algorithm) would let other benchmark authors cite it directly — right now it is prose, and prose does not get cited.
14. **Test the remote-depot hypothesis.** The paper correctly identifies that its central finding may be an artifact of depot geometry and correctly says it cannot test this (both networks share the geometry). A single supplementary scenario — same bins, depot relocated into the service area, even for one constructor pair — would convert the paper's biggest confound into its second headline finding. The simulator already supports it.
15. **Report the frontier, not just points on it.** The strategy stage's monotone efficiency-service frontier is the paper's main result; an explicit frontier plot with per-configuration clouds behind the five variant means (the data exists) would make the "no dominant variant" conclusion visual and memorable.
16. **Lead the abstract and introduction with the selection-dominance finding** once the NCO framing is repaired — it is the sentence this paper has earned the right to say.

## 7. Overall Assessment

| Dimension | Grade | Comment |
|---|---|---|
| Technical soundness of experiment | B+ | Design is sound; improver stage broken; single realization limits inference |
| Statistical integrity | A | Best-in-class for the genre; independently verified, error-free |
| Reporting completeness | C+ | Objective never reported; parameters missing; kg/lost absent |
| Internal consistency | B− | One critical contradiction (sensing), one abstract-body gap (NCO), several minor |
| Writing quality | A− | Excellent prose; overlong; some redundancy; a handful of typos |
| Figures/tables | B− (verified aspects) | Generated, consistent tables; PNG table, raster figures, outlier-hostaged axes |
| Related work | A− | Accurate, well-organized, appropriately scoped |
| Motivation & framing | B | Strong opening; undermined by abstract mismatch |
| Reproducibility | C | No code link; missing all numeric parameters; "reproducible" claim unsupported in-text |
| Significance of contribution | B+ | Framework + benchmark + a genuinely useful negative-space finding (selection > construction) |

**Final verdict**: Major revision. The scientific core — paired simulation, integrity-aware analysis, the three-stage decomposition, and the selection-dominance finding — is solid and unusually well-guarded against self-deception. What stands between this paper and acceptance at a good OR venue is not more research but discipline in framing: make the abstract describe the paper that exists, make the protocol state the parameters the experiments used, report the objective the solvers optimize, and release the code the title advertises. The authors have already done the hard part — being rigorous with themselves. They now need to be equally rigorous with how the work is presented.
