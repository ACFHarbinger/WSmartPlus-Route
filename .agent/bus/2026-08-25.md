#### The 90-day grid is not a grid

Runs per constructor, 30d vs 90d:

| constructor | 30d | 90d |
|---|---|---|
| ACO_HH | 60 | 30 |
| ALNS | 60 | **0** |
| BPC | 60 | 60 |
| HGS | 60 | 12 |
| PG-CLNS | 60 | 42 |
| PSOMA | 60 | 12 |
| SANS | 60 | 12 |
| SWC-TCF | 60 | **6** |

The 30-day design is complete and perfectly balanced (60 each, every
constructor x strategy cell filled). The 90-day one is not: ALNS was never
run at all, SWC-TCF got 6 of 60, and HGS/PSOMA/SANS 12. **A cross-constructor
ranking at 90 days would be comparing different subsets of scenarios and is
not sound.** The paper will lead on the 30-day grid and use 90 days only for
the horizon-robustness question on the subset that is actually covered
(BPC and PG-CLNS, with ACO_HH partial) — stated as such.

#### One run is corrupt and poisons three aggregates

`SWC-TCF / LA / Gamma-3 / N=350` reports 2168 overflows and 18,831 kg lost.
It is not a bad policy — **the simulation stopped at day 15 of 30**
(`days=15`, `kg=37,065` against ~70,300 kg for all seven other constructors
in that identical cell). It ran out of budget halfway and the remaining 15
days of waste simply piled up.

Averaged in, it single-handedly produces the "LA has 51.3 mean overflows"
and "SWC-TCF has 99.7 mean overflows" numbers. Both are artefacts. The two
affected rows (CLS and FTSP) are excluded from aggregate statistics and
reported separately as a scale limit of the two-commodity-flow MIP.

Second, milder case in the same cell: `HGS + CLS` drives 21,832 km where
every other constructor is at 7,000–13,500, dropping its kg/km to 3.28.
Unlike the SWC-TCF row this one ran all 27 days and collected full tonnage,
so it is a real result — an HGS route-length pathology under CLS — not a
truncation. Kept, and called out.

#### Results that are clean and hold up

From the balanced 30-day grid (480 runs, outlier rows excluded):

- **Route improver: CLS beats Fast-TSP, decisively.** Paired on all 240
  configurations that differ only in improver, CLS wins 212/240 on kg/km,
  mean +0.65 kg/km, and produces shorter routes (4821 vs 4981 km mean) at
  indistinguishable runtime. The paper currently draws no conclusion here at
  all.
- **The selection strategies are a clean efficiency/service trade-off**, and
  the threshold variants order monotonically:

  | variant | kg/km | overflows | km | ncol |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | LM-CF90 | 7.74 | 28.6 | 3725 | 2141 |
  | LM-CF70 | 6.23 | 7.7 | 4646 | 2721 |
  | SL-SL1 | 5.65 | 6.3 | 5088 | 3037 |
  | SL-SL2 | 4.25 | 9.3 | 6691 | 4030 |

  Collect later and you haul more per kilometre and overflow more. That is
  the paper's central operational message and it is visible in one table.
- **ACO-HH is the runtime/quality sweet spot**: 6.39 kg/km (3rd of 8, within
  5% of the best) at 621 s mean — 2× faster than ALNS, 4× faster than HGS.
  The hyper-heuristic pays off.
- **BPC leads on kg/km** (6.73) and is mid-pack on time (1207 s).
- **Gamma-3 is a much heavier load than the empirical data**: 35.4 vs 5.9
  mean overflows, 268 vs 21 kg lost. Conclusions must be stated per
  distribution; pooling them is misleading.
