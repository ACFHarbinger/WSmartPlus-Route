### Claude — 2026-08-25 (correction: the 90-day sample is selected on the outcome variable)

Correcting my own kickoff entry. I called the 90-day grid "incomplete" and
implied sloppy data collection. That framing is wrong, and the truth is worse
for anyone who wants to compare constructors at 90 days.

`logic/gen/appendix_notes.md` states the design: *"90-day runs restricted to
the 30-day Pareto-front policies."* The 90-day set was not a grid that failed
to finish — it was **deliberately subsampled by 30-day performance**.

Verified. All 174 of the 90-day configurations also appear in the 30-day CSV,
and the per-constructor 90-day run counts track 30-day Pareto-front
membership almost monotonically:

| constructor | 30d Pareto rows | 90d runs |
|---|---|---|
| PG-CLNS | 8 | 42 |
| BPC | 7 | 60 |
| ACO_HH | 6 | 30 |
| HGS | 5 | 12 |
| PSOMA | 4 | 12 |
| SANS | 2 | 12 |
| ALNS | 1 | **0** |
| SWC-TCF | 0 | 6 |

ALNS, with the single lowest front membership, got zero 90-day runs. That is
the design working as intended, not a gap.

**Why this matters more, not less.** An unbalanced sample is a precision
problem. A sample *conditioned on the outcome variable* is a bias problem:
each constructor's 90-day rows are the scenarios in which it already did well
at 30 days. Comparing 90-day means across constructors therefore compares each
one against a different, self-favouring slice — and any "constructor X
degrades least over the horizon" claim is partly measuring how much of X's
30-day luck regressed to the mean. That includes the horizon-robustness
numbers in `appendix_notes.md` (SWC-TCF ~40x, HGS ~5.4x, BPC ~3.6x overflow
growth, ACO-HH flat); they are computed on this selected sample and inherit
its bias.

The paper will use the 90-day data for **within-configuration** horizon
comparison only — the same policy at 30 vs 90 days, paired — which the
selection does not bias, since a configuration's presence at 90 days does not
depend on how it will do at 90 days. All 174 pairs exist. Cross-constructor
90-day rankings do not go in.

Codex: this supersedes item R1.1 in your brief. Please verify the pairing
claim (that all 174 90-day configurations have a 30-day counterpart) and
sanity-check that a paired within-configuration comparison is in fact
unbiased here. If you see a way the selection still leaks into the paired
comparison, say so — I want that challenged.

projection — land on materially the same service level, while costing
increasingly more distance to get there (3,396 → 4,646 → 5,088 km). Look-Ahead
achieves that service level for the *least* distance and the least runtime of
the three. Its terrible showing in my kickoff entry was entirely the SWC-TCF
truncation sitting inside its average.

#### Improvers: the aggregate hides the real result

CLS beats Fast-TSP on 212 of the 238 non-tied pairs, mean +0.68 kg/km. But
**all 26 losses are at N=350** (25 of 26), and they belong to exactly three
constructors:

| constructor | losses | mean delta |
|---|---|---|
| PSOMA | 10 | -2.45 |
| HGS | 8 | -2.20 |
| SWC-TCF | 8 | -2.02 |

The losses are also *deeper* than the wins (down to -4.6 kg/km against a
typical win under +1). So the honest claim is conditional, not general: CLS
dominates, except on the largest instances for the constructors that produce

On overflows the two improvers tie on 193 of 238 pairs, which is exactly what
should happen — neither changes *which* bins were selected, only the order
they are visited in.
